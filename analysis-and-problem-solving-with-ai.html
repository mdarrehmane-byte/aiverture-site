How to Use AI for Analysis & Problem Solving

Break down tricky problems and decisions, step by step — starting from the very basics.

Aiverture · Updated July 2026 · 5 min read

Some problems are hard not because they're complicated, but because they're tangled — too many factors to hold in your head at once. This is where AI genuinely shines: as a thinking partner that helps you untangle things.

What this actually looks like in practice

Think of AI here less like a calculator and more like a whiteboard session with someone patient. You describe the situation, and it helps you break it into pieces, weigh tradeoffs, or work through the logic step by step.

Real examples to try

Weighing a decision

"Help me think through two job offers — one pays more, one has better hours. Walk me through what actually matters here."

Working through math or logic

"Walk me through this problem step by step, don't just give me the answer."

Untangling a messy situation

"My monthly budget isn't working and I don't know why — help me think through where the leaks might be."

Stress-testing an idea

"I'm thinking about starting a small side business — what am I not considering?"

How to get better results

  • Give the full picture, not just the question. Constraints matter — budget, time, what you've already tried. The more real context, the more useful the answer.
  • Ask it to show its reasoning. "Walk me through how you got there" often reveals the actual thinking, not just a final answer you have to trust blindly.
  • Push back. If something doesn't sit right, say so — "but what about X?" AI genuinely responds well to being challenged.
Worth knowing: AI is a strong thinking partner, not a decision-maker. For anything with real stakes — money, health, relationships — use it to think more clearly, then make the call yourself.
