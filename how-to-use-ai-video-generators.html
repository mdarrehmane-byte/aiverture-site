How to Use AI Video Generators

What AI video can actually do today, real examples, and honest limitations — starting from the very basics.

Aiverture · Updated July 2026 · 5 min read

AI video has moved fast, but it's still not "type anything, get a flawless movie." Here's a realistic picture of what it's actually good for right now.

What AI video generators actually do well today

Short clips (a few seconds to a couple minutes), simple scenes, stylized or abstract visuals, and turning existing footage or images into something new. Tools like Runway and Pika excel here — think b-roll, social clips, and creative experiments, not full productions.

Real examples to try

Generating from text

"A slow-motion shot of waves crashing on a rocky shore at sunset, cinematic lighting"

Animating a still image

Upload a photo and ask the tool to bring it to life with subtle motion

Avatar-style video

Turn a script into a talking avatar video using a tool like HeyGen — useful for training videos or narrated content without being on camera

How to get better results

  • Keep it simple. Short, clear scenes generate more reliably than complex multi-action sequences.
  • Expect to generate a few times. The first result is rarely the final one — treat early attempts as drafts.
  • Combine with a real video editor. AI-generated clips often work best as raw material you edit together, like in CapCut, rather than a finished standalone video.
Honest limitation: AI video still struggles with longer, complex, or highly precise scenes, and results can look slightly "off" in ways that are hard to predict. It's genuinely useful, just not yet a replacement for traditional filming when precision matters.
